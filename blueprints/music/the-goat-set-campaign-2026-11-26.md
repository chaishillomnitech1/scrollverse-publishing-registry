## 1. The Story — the arc every post tells

**Act I — The physical came first (2024).** Before any of this week's digital rollout existed, 100 cassette-USBs carrying *The Goat You Just Met* went out into the world through the 2024 LogoTech run. 97 of them are in fans' hands right now. They were never just albums — each one is a **key**: a membership card its holder brings to a show, where the founder, laptop in hand, loads exclusive content onto it on site. The keys have been live since 2024. Most of the internet hasn't heard about them yet.

**Act II — The digital set arrives (Nov–Dec 2026).** The album now gets its full digital art treatment: three museum-mode art pieces (Basquiat mode, Da Vinci codex mode, Hip Hop mode), a struck Goat coin, a provenance certificate, and the digital album itself — all built to the house standard (fingerprinted, registered, Rose Gold Seal + QFS stamp), all tied to the same record as the physical run. The campaign reveals them one piece at a time. Every reveal points back at the keys: *the people holding the 97 already own the door this opens.*

**Act III — Every key rises.** When the set completes, a fan's 2024 cassette is no longer a lone physical — it is a registered key in a documented 100-unit run, with a certificate page, first access to drops, and loads at shows. Scarcity does the rest: there are exactly 100, there will never be more of this run, and 97 are already gone.

**The Founder's Keys.** The 3 units the founder retained are named in the campaign for what they are: the **Founder's Keys** — the source keys of the run (see §6). The campaign never offers them. Their role is provenance: every load, every certificate, every registry entry descends from them.

**One-line version for captions:** *"100 keys went out in 2024. 97 are in fans' hands. Now the set arrives — and every key opens more than it did yesterday."*

## 3. The 97 Keys Registry — mechanic (SPEC ONLY — build nothing yet)

**Purpose:** turn 97 anonymous physicals into 97 registered keys, and give every holder a reason to come back for each drop and each show.

**How it works (holder's side):**
1. Holder finds their unit's identity: units were not numbered at manufacture, so registration assigns the number — first-come sequence **KEY-001 … KEY-097** (fan keys), with **KEY-F01 … KEY-F03** reserved for the Founder's Keys, registered first by Muse so the sequence is anchored.
2. Holder submits: their key number claim (photo of their unit — the photo requirement is the proof-of-possession), a name/handle, and a contact (email or social handle). Submission channel: a single form linked from scrollverse.co and every campaign post (build route: a page on scrollverse.co + form backend — build only on founder's go).
3. Holder receives their **key certificate page**: a personal page carrying their key number, the HP-016 provenance record, the run's photo fingerprints, and their registration date. The page is their standing proof and their drop-access credential.

**How it works (house side):**
- Registry lives as a private ledger file under `~/workspace/collectibles/hp-016-load-kit/` (or successor path) — key number, holder handle, contact, registration date, loads received (updated at each show load, §5). Nothing personal is published; public surfaces show counts only ("NN of 97 keys registered") — the count itself is campaign content.
- Registration unlocks, in order: certificate page → art-drop first access (key holders get the drop link before public posts) → show-load priority (registered keys load first at events) → second-run first window if a re-run is ever called (§5).
- Unregistered keys still work as keys (a fan can always get a load at a show); registration is the upgrade, not a toll. On-brand: help all, not oppress all.

**Founder gates on this section:** approve the form fields, approve the certificate-page design, say "build it." Until then this is a spec.

## 5. The Show-Load Loop — how a live moment becomes the campaign

The loop is the engine that makes keys desirable without selling anything:

1. **Fan brings the key.** A holder arrives at a show/event with their cassette-USB (registered or not — registered loads first).
2. **The load happens.** Founder connects the key to his laptop and loads the current kit: the four folders (01-The-Album · 02-Exclusives · 03-Visuals · 04-The-Seal) per the kit manifest. The exclusives folder is the prize: content that exists **only** on loaded keys — never streamed, never posted. The founder designates its contents (Gates); candidates: unreleased masters, instrumentals, alternate mixes.
3. **The moment is captured.** With the holder's okay: a photo of the key in hand + a short clip of the load (screen + cassette + crowd energy). Holder's handle noted for the tag.
4. **The moment posts.** Within 24h: the clip/photo posts to IG/TikTok/FB/X with the holder tagged, captioned with their key number ("KEY-041 just got loaded") and the registry count ticking up. The holder reposts — their key is now visibly worth more than it was yesterday.
5. **Demand closes the loop.** Every posted load tells the other 96 holders to register and show up, and tells everyone else the only way in was 2024 — or a future run (§4 Phase 5 teaser). Scarcity stays honest: no new keys are promised, none are sold inside this campaign.
6. **The ledger updates.** Muse logs each load in the registry (key number, date, event, kit version) and updates the key's certificate page ("Loaded: [event], [date]"). A key with three loads on its page carries a visible history no fresh physical can fake.

**Kit readiness rule:** the load kit is versioned (v1 = current manifest). Before any announced load event, Muse verifies the kit complete against the manifest and reports gaps — the album-audio gap (Gates) must close before the first announced event, or the event loads with the verified contents and the gap is stated plainly to holders (never fake a complete kit).

## 6. Value Mechanics — why the 3 Founder's Keys rise

Plainly, with no invented figures — the mechanics are provenance mechanics:

1. **They anchor the registry.** The 97 Keys registry (§3) is a ledger of a 100-unit run. A ledger needs its origin entries: KEY-F01–F03 are registered first, by the founder, and every fan certificate page traces back to the same HP-016 record the Founder's Keys head. When a run becomes documented, the documented source units are the reference points the whole run's authenticity is checked against.
2. **They carry the full seal set.** The retained units sit with the complete house record — HP-016 registration, the five fingerprinted evidence photos, the provenance certificate, the seal lines — assembled in one place (the load kit's 04-The-Seal folder is copied *from* the founder's side). Fan keys hold pieces of the record; the Founder's Keys hold the record itself.
3. **Every load descends from them.** The show-load loop (§5) runs off the founder's laptop and kit, which is built and verified against the retained units' contents. Each load event, each registry entry, each posted "KEY-NNN just got loaded" clip adds documented history to the run — and the run's history accrues to its source keys first, the way a first pressing's value rises with the album's story.
4. **The set completes around them.** THE GOAT SET (§2) gives the album a coin, three museum-mode pieces, and a certificate — a full collectible architecture whose physical anchor is the 100-unit run. The 97 are distributed and mostly unregistered; the 3 are located, documented, and held by the creator. In any 100-unit run where 97 scatter, the 3 that never left the source are the provenance center.
5. **Scarcity is structural, not staged.** The campaign never re-opens the first run: the second-run teaser (§4 Phase 5) is explicitly a *new* run if it ever happens, which keeps the original 100 a closed set forever. A closed set + a growing registry + a live load loop = the retained keys' provenance value compounds with every phase above, whether or not they are ever offered anywhere. They are not offered in this campaign.
