# HIT-SONG BLUEPRINTS 2026
## 19 Nov 2026 — for the 7-song Suno build (Chais The Great)
## Research only — no songs generated. Sources cited in the analyst's report; key sources listed at the end.

## 0. UNIVERSAL RULES (apply to all 7 songs)

- **Spotify counts a stream only after 30 seconds.** A skip before 0:30 earns nothing and signals low engagement.
- **Hook element within the first 5–15 seconds; first full chorus by ~0:30–0:45.** If the hook is not in place by 0:15, you are gambling with the royalty check.
- **Three choruses.** 69% of 2024 Billboard Hot 100 No. 1 hits featured three full choruses.
- **Intro is 2–4 bars (5–10 sec), not 20–40 sec.** Traditional 8–16 bar intros are dead for commercial singles.
- **Length:** Billboard Year-End Hot 100 2025 averaged 3:24 (median 3:13). The 2:30–3:30 window is the streaming sweet spot.
- **Title arrives ~54 seconds in** on average across No. 1 records.
- **The 15-second TikTok moment:** build the song around one snippet that lives on short-form video; make the outro loop cleanly back into the intro so replays stack.
- **Proof of the formula:** Tate McRae "Greedy" — 2:11, 8-second intro, first chorus inside the 30-second window, three full chorus repetitions.

## 1. RAP — "Crown Frequency" (92 BPM, ~3:30)
- Ideal duration 2:30–3:00 (streaming rap lives here; spec runs slightly long — watch the tail). BPM: trap 130–145 (half-time feel 65–72); boom-bap/lyrical 85–100. 92 BPM sits in the lyrical pocket.
- Structure (hook-first streaming layout): Intro 4 bars → Hook 8 → Verse 1 (12) → Hook 8 → Verse 2 (12) → Hook 8 → Outro 4 = 56 bars. All sections in multiples of 4.
- Hook placement: inside the first 15 seconds — hook-first is the dominant streaming layout.
- Production: 808 + hi-hat groove in the 4-bar intro; 12-bar verses are the modern radio default; hook = 8 bars, one or two central phrases repeated with variation.
- Proof: Lil Baby "Dead Fresh" — No. 1 Billboard Hot Rap Songs, week of 21 Nov 2026.

## 2. R&B — "Water Calms Me" (78 BPM, ~3:45)
- Ideal duration 2:30–3:30. BPM 60–100 (contemporary 90–100; neo-soul 70–85). 78 BPM is the neo-soul pocket.
- Structure: Intro 4–8 → Verse 1 (8–16) → Pre-Chorus 4–8 → Chorus 8–16 → Verse 2 → Chorus → Bridge 8 → Final Chorus → Extended Outro 4–8+ (ad-libs/runs).
- Hook placement: first chorus before ~0:45–1:00 — R&B gets slightly more room because the groove carries it.
- Production: jazz-derived harmony (ii7–V7–Imaj7–vi7, extended 7ths/9ths); space is a compositional tool — a two-beat pause before the hook, a bar with the kick dropped; change one small element every 4–8 bars so a slow beat never sits still.
- Proof: Bruno Mars "I Just Might" — 36 weeks at No. 1 on Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs, the longest reign in the chart's history.

## 3. GOSPEL — "Agape (The Fire Heals)" (100 BPM, ~4:00)
- Ideal duration 3:00–4:30 for a radio/streaming cut (gospel runs longer — the vamp earns the extra minute). No verified chart-average length exists for gospel singles — flagged unverified.
- BPM by sub-style: hymns/slow worship 60–80; traditional choir 80–110; contemporary 90–120; praise & worship 90–130; shout 120–160. 100 BPM = contemporary gospel.
- Structure: Intro (organ/piano establishes key) → Verse 1 (lead calls, choir answers) → Chorus (full choir, tambourine, claps) → Verse 2 → Chorus → Praise break → Vamp (repeated phrase, key changes climbing upward) → Peak → Outro.
- Hook placement: the chorus declaration lands early (first ~0:45) and then REPEATS as the vamp — gospel's hook is a short, singable, repeatable line.
- Production: Hammond B3 organ is mandatory — name it first. SATB four-part choir (4–8 vocal tracks minimum, hall reverb 2.5–4s); [Lead]/[Choir] tags for call-and-response; tambourine on every 8th note + claps on 2 and 4; Vsus4–V7–I resolution; keys Bb/Eb most common; dynamics are everything — start sparse, big final key change.
- Suno note: the gospel guide stresses naming the organ first and using [Lead]/[Choir] tags.
- Proof: Renee Spearman "You Chose Me" — No. 1 Billboard Gospel Airplay, chart dated 7 Nov 2026.

## 4. AFROBEATS — "ScrollVerse Nights" (108 BPM, ~3:20)
- Ideal duration 2:30–3:30. BPM 95–115, most records 100–108; median 110 (Mixgraph, 242 tracks). 108 BPM is dead center.
- Structure: Intro (percussion groove, 4–8 bars) → Verse 1 (short, stripped back) → Pre-Chorus (call-and-response lift) → Chorus → Instrumental/Percussion Break → Verse 2 → Final Chorus → Outro (groove fades).
- Hook placement: hook melody inside the first minute — "the topline early." The hook is a short vowel-rich chant phrase (2–5 syllables, open vowels) people can sing before they know the verses.
- Production: the 3+3+2 groove — group the bar's eight eighth-notes as 3+3+2, kicks on the 1st, 4th and 7th eighth-notes. Syncopated kick, wooden rimshot, shakers, congas, log drum or soft 808, warm round bass, bright highlife-style guitar licks; 2–4 repeating chords; leave the middle of the mix empty for the vocal; arrange in 8-bar blocks, add/subtract one layer at a time.
- Proof: Asake "Remember" — the most-streamed song ever on Spotify Nigeria.

## 5. LO-FI — "Midnight Atlas" (72 BPM, ~2:45)
- Ideal duration 2:00–3:00 (average 2:30). Playlist economics reward short, loopable tracks. BPM 70–90, majority 75–85, classic head-nod 78–85. 72 BPM is just below the classic pocket — on the ambient edge, fine for the frequency lane.
- Structure: Intro 4–8 bars (chords alone, filtered, crackle up) → Main loop 16 bars → Variation 8–16 bars → Breakdown 4–8 bars (drums out) → Return 16 bars → Outro 4–8 bars (strip back, fade on noise + last chord). Arrangement is subtraction, not addition.
- Hook placement: there is no pop hook — the LOOP is the hook. The 4-bar loop must be recognizable within ~11–12 seconds. The "payoff" is a small variation every 16 bars, not a chorus. Do not force a verse/chorus shape onto it.
- Production: swing 55–62% on 16ths; jazz chords — maj7/min7/dom9, ii–V–I and I–vi–IV–V are the two master loops; Rhodes/soft piano; deliberate degradation — vinyl crackle, tape saturation/wobble, high-frequency roll-off; round bass following chord roots; mix soft and restrained.
- Proof: lo-fi does not chart on the Hot 100 — its hits are playlist-driven. Lofi Fruits Music "I'm Yours" — 74.3M Spotify streams.

## 6. CINEMATIC / SCORE — "Thoth Hawk Ascends" (120 BPM building, ~3:00)
- Ideal duration 1:30–3:30 for a trailer cue; ~2:30 is the working standard.
- BPM is an arc, not a number: 60–80 (intro) → 80–110 rising → 110–140 (climax) → 60–80 returning. 120 BPM is the trailer favorite (1 bar = exactly 2 seconds).
- Structure (three-act trailer arc): Phase 1 "The Whisper" 0:00–0:30 (solo instrument, sparse) → Phase 2 "The Awakening" 0:30–1:00 (rhythm arrives) → Phase 3 "The Rise" 1:00–1:40 (layers stack every 8 bars) → Phase 4 "The Climax" 1:40–2:15 (FULL FORCE — brass, full choir, pounding percussion, key change minor→major) → Phase 5 "The Resolution" 2:15–2:45 (decrescendo, 1–2 ending hits for the title card).
- Hook placement: the "hook" is the climax hit at ~1:40–2:15 — but the retention hook is the opening 30 seconds: a signature sound must be established immediately.
- Production: minor keys throughout (D minor → D major is the classic dark-to-triumphant move); dynamics ppp→fff→p; keep vocals off for underscore; leave holes in the climax for dialogue/SFX; end on a hard stop, silence, then one last hit.
- Suno note: attach a concrete parameter to every adjective ("epic" = "90 BPM, D minor, full orchestra").
- Proof: cinematic does not chart — its hits are trailer placements and sync licenses (Two Steps from Hell, Audiomachine, Hans Zimmer as reference points).

## 7. SOUL (NEO-SOUL) — "Ancestor's Light" (84 BPM, ~3:50)
- Ideal duration 3:00–4:00 (soul earns a longer runtime; the outro vamp is part of the song). No verified chart-average length exists — flagged unverified.
- BPM 65–100 (classic neo-soul 70–85). 84 BPM is the neo-soul pocket.
- Structure: Intro 4–8 (Rhodes/piano solo) → Verse 1 (8–16) → Pre-Chorus 4–8 → Chorus 8 (horns enter, harmonies thick) → Verse 2 → Bridge 8–16 (harmonic departure, key change or vamp) → Outro 8–16+ (vamp, vocal improvisation, fade or cold end).
- Hook placement: chorus arrives ~0:45–1:00 — soul is the one genre allowed a slower first payoff, because the groove and vocal intimacy hold the listener. The emotional peak is the bridge/outro vamp, not the first chorus.
- Production: live instruments — Rhodes, upright bass, acoustic drums; jazz harmony — extended chords, Dorian mode; space and dynamics over volume; outro vamp with vocal improvisation is mandatory.
- Proof: SZA "Snooze" — 68 BPM, Grammy-winning, 24-second atmospheric intro, sparse verses, deep bassline, restrained chorus, gentle fade-out.

## ELECTRONIC (alternate) — "Light the Dark" (128 BPM, ~3:15)
- Duration 2:30–3:30 (radio edit). BPM 118–132 (house 120–130, big room 126–132). 128 BPM is the festival pocket.
- Structure: Intro 16–32 bars → Build 1 (16) → Drop 1 (16–32) → Breakdown 16–32 → Build 2 (16) → Drop 2 (16–32) → Outro 16–32. The hook IS the drop; first drop by ~0:45–1:00 in a radio edit.
- Proof: Tame Impala & JENNIE "Dracula" — Hot 100 #3, week of 21 Nov 2026.
