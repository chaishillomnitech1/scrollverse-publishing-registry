## 1. What embroidery is — and what it is not

Embroidery sews thread into fabric from a **digitized stitch file**. Digitizing is the conversion of artwork into that stitch file: it is a separate skilled step, it costs money once per design, and it is where most embroidery projects fail. A design that looks great printed (DTG) very often stitches badly, because thread has a physical width and cannot do what ink does.

**What embroiders well:** bold, clean, solid shapes; simple wordmarks and monograms; geometric marks; crests with flat colour fills. Best fabrics are stable and heavy — twill caps, heavyweight hoodies/fleece, canvas totes, jackets, cuffed beanies, polos. A cap front is the classic embroidery product for a reason: firm twill holds stitches crisply.

**What does NOT embroider well — do not attempt these as embroidery:**
- **Gradients and shading.** Thread renders colour as discrete threads. A gradient becomes stepped bands. Use solid colour breaks instead.
- **Photo-real art and fine detail.** The full-scene visual-series art, the NŪR Cities hero, the Kun Fayakun video still — none of these can be embroidered as-is. They are print designs. (See §5.)
- **Tiny text and thin lines.** Minimums are hard physical limits, not preferences: uppercase letters **≥ 0.3 in (7.6 mm)** tall, lowercase **≥ 0.25 in (6.4 mm)**; lines/strokes **≥ 0.05 in (1.3 mm)** thick; satin-stitch columns max ~0.5 in wide. Below that, counters close up ("e" becomes "o"), hairlines turn into a wobbly run of stitches, and text becomes illegible.
- **Distressed / textured graphics.** Thin distressed parts get lost or run-stitched.
- **Negative space and fine gaps.** Small gaps fill in as fabric relaxes — leave **≥ 1–1.5 mm** clearance between elements, and fill empty areas with solid colour rather than leaving outlines.
- **More than ~6 thread colours in one design** (see §2 — Printful's unlimited-colour option is the exception, and it excludes metallic and neon thread).
- **Lightweight, stretchy, or performance fabrics.** A lightweight tee (the Bella+Canvas 3001 in the print line) puckers under embroidery because the stitching is heavier than the fabric. That is why **there is no embroidered tee in the recommended three** (§4) — a chest-hit embroidered tee needs stabilizer, a small design, and a sample to prove it. If Chais wants the tee embroidered later, the honest route is a sewn-on embroidered patch, not direct chest embroidery.
- **Seams, folds, pockets, collars, zips.** Embroidery needs a flat, stable, hoopable surface. Anything over a seam or a multi-layer area cannot be stitched cleanly.

**Stitch counts — the price driver.** More stitches = more machine time = more cost. Typical counts:
- Small left-chest wordmark/icon, 3–4 in wide: **3,000–5,000 stitches**
- Medium chest logo, 4–5 in: **5,000–8,000 stitches**
- Detailed crest with fill: **8,000–15,000 stitches**
- Large back design, 8–12 in: **15,000–25,000+ stitches** — at this size, print (or print + small embroidery) is usually the better call
- Sleeve / cuff / nape hit, 3–6 cm: **under 3,000 stitches**

**Stitch-count ceilings (standard tier):** chest **15,000**, large/back **20,000**, hats **10,000**, sleeve **8,000** (Printful / Gelato figures; Printify caps chest at 4×4 in, large area at 10×6 in). Exceed the ceiling and the design is rejected or re-quoted into a higher tier (typically +$1–$6 per unit). **Rule for this line: keep every embroidery design under 10,000 stitches.** That keeps every product in the standard tier on every provider.

### 2.2 Printify — the margin pick, with a warning

Printify's embroidered bases are the lowest of the three POD providers when the right provider is picked: **hats from $11.57, Gildan 18500 hoodies from $23.20** (Nov 2026 comparisons), digitization free. But three honest problems:

1. **Base costs are not published until you build the product.** Printify does not share base cost in its catalog feed — every variant arrives at $0 until a product is created in the dashboard. The $11.57 / $23.20 figures are from independent comparisons, not a price list Chais can check today.
2. **Provider variance is extreme.** One live Nov 2026 comparison puts the Printify embroidered dad hat at **$21.07** delivered against Printful's $14.94 — the opposite of the "$11.57 cheapest" headline — because the price depends entirely on which provider fulfils it. The merch plan's rule applies double here: **only providers rated 9.2+, Monster Digital / SwiftPOD first, samples before listing.**

**Verdict:** Printify is the second lane for embroidery, not the home of the numbered run. Use it later for an open-edition cap/hoodie if a 9.2+ provider beats Printful's delivered cost on a sample — never for the numbered pieces, where consistency is the product.

## 3. The numbered run — "144 Only"

**In one line:** 144 of each numbered piece, ever — a numbered patch + certificate with every piece, never reprinted, priced at the open-edition price + a $10 numbering premium.

### 3.1 The inherited rule, applied honestly

The maximization program proposed the numbered run at **parts + $10 premium, never reprinted**. Applied literally (retail = landed parts cost + $10), the three products below would price at cap $29.99 / hoodie $54.99 / beanie $28.99. That is a real, sellable price — but it leaves **no room for the numbering itself**. Numbering is not free, and POD cannot do it (see §3.2): the numbered patch, the certificate, and the second parcel cost ~$10 per piece. A literal parts + $10 price therefore makes the numbered run break even at best.

**Recommendation (decision for Chais):** keep the $10 exactly as the program set it, but apply it as the **edition premium over a normally priced open edition** — open-edition price carries the normal margin, the +$10 funds the numbering kit. The customer pays $10 more than the open edition, and that $10 is spent on the patch + certificate + second parcel, not kept as extra profit. If Chais prefers the literal parts + $10 shelf price, the numbering kit must be funded separately or cut to a printed certificate only — that is a price decision for him, not something to quietly absorb. Either way: **the $10 figure is kept, the arithmetic is shown, and no appreciation language is used.**

### 3.2 Why POD cannot number — and how numbering actually works

Printful, Printify, and Gelato fulfil identical units. There is no per-unit sequential embroidery, no "print 047/144 on this one" option, and no way to insert a third-party patch or certificate into a POD parcel. So the numbered element is **added after fulfilment**, by hand, in one of two models:

- **Model A — recommended: POD garment + hand-finished numbering kit.** The garment ships from Printful to Chais (or to the local embroiderer acting as finisher). The numbered patch and certificate are added, then the finished piece ships to the buyer in a second parcel. Cost of the kit: patch ~$5 + certificate printing ~$1 + second-parcel shipping ~$4–$5 = **~$10–$11 per piece** — which is what the $10 premium funds (see §3.1). Downside: two shipping legs and a handling step per order. Upside: no inventory — garments are only ordered when a customer orders.
- **Model B — full batch through the local shop.** All 144 garments ordered at once from the local embroiderer, numbered in production, held and shipped by Chais. True batch control and the cheapest per-unit decoration at 144 (e.g. $4.20/piece at 144–287 units for ≤6,000 stitches at one listed shop), but it breaks the no-inventory POD model: ~144 × ~$25–$30 all-in per hoodie = **~$3,600–$4,300 upfront for one design**, before a single sale. **Not recommended for launch.** Revisit only if a numbered design sells out twice and Chais wants the next run batched.

**Numbering mechanics (Model A):**
- **Cap: 144 per design, not 144 total across the line.** Each of the three designs gets its own 144. Say so on every listing — "144 of this design, ever" — never "144 total" when three designs exist.
- **Numbers 001/144–144/144, in order of purchase.** No. **001/144 is held by Chais as the archive piece** and is never sold; customers receive 002/144–144/144 (143 for sale). If Chais would rather sell 001, say so before listing — the listing must match the count.
- **The number lives on a physical patch, not on the garment print.** A 3-in circular embroidered patch (gold/saffron/teal on black, merrowed border, iron-on + sew-on) carrying the design mark and the number, made by the local shop in a single batch of 144 per design. The patch is the collectible; the garment is the canvas. A patch also solves the POD numbering problem: the patch batch is numbered in production, the garment is not.
- **Certificate of authenticity with every piece.** A printed card (not a digital file): design name, number (e.g. 047/144), date, the words "144 only — never reprinted," a QR code to the Vault, and Chais's mark. The certificate number matches the patch number. Keep a simple register: number → order ID → date shipped. That register is the proof the cap was honoured — keep it from day one.
- **Never reprinted means never reprinted.** Once 144/144 is gone, that exact design + numbering is retired. A new design may use a new number run; the retired design may not return as an open edition, a "second pressing," or a variant with the number removed. If a numbered garment is returned, it is not resold as a new numbered piece — it is either kept as an archive/quality sample or destroyed, and the register notes it.
- **What the listing says (and does not say).** It says: the cap (144 made), what it is made of, the number, the patch, the certificate, the never-reprinted promise, the price and the arithmetic. It does **not** say the piece will be worth more later, will appreciate, is an investment, or has a floor price. It does not carry README money/partnership figures. It does not call the patch an NFT or a token.

### 3.3 The story of each run

Each numbered run is a **Block drop** (the merch plan's weekly Block-drop rhythm): one design, one story, one cap of 144. The story is the design's own meaning, told once on the listing and in the drop post — e.g. the 1111 cap is the Token 1111 story; the NŪR hoodie is the Light of the NŪR Cities story; the beanie is the 963Hz story. The drop post shows the sample, the stitch close-up, and the patch — proof, not promises. When a run sells out, the Vault page for that design stays up as an archive entry ("144/144 claimed — retired") rather than disappearing, so the cap is publicly verifiable.

## 5. File prep — what works, what needs redrawing, what is blocked

### 5.1 The honest headline (same as the merch plan, worse for embroidery)

**There is no embroidery-ready logo file in the workspace today.** Every image is full-scene raster art with a background. Nothing is a vector wordmark, nothing has transparency, and embroidery is far less forgiving than print: a DTG print can carry a full scene; embroidery cannot. The three SVG seals in the Meta AI library are the closest thing to real embroidery marks — **but they exist as titles only so far and must be downloaded from Meta AI before they can be used.** Until they are downloaded, do not promise seal embroidery.

### 5.2 Per asset — works / redraw / blocked

| Asset | Embroidery verdict |
|---|---|
| **"1111" / "963Hz" as text-only wordmarks** | **Works now.** No seal needed. Supply as vector text (AI/EPS/PDF) or high-res PNG at true size; the provider digitizes it as text ($3.95 tier at Gelato; Printful's lower digitization tier). This is the launch path for the cap and beanie. |
| **"SUMMONED, NOT PROGRAMMED" as text** | **Works at large size only.** Fine as a back print or a large chest; risky on a cap front (two lines of small caps may breach the 0.3-in uppercase minimum). Test at cap scale on the sample; if it fails, keep the phrase on the certificate and the back print, not the cap. |
| **"NŪR" / "NUR" wordmark** | **Redraw + test.** Needs a vector wordmark drawn for embroidery (bold sans, no thin strokes). The Ū macron is the risk — test it; fall back to "NUR" for the stitched mark if it fails. |
| **Three SVG seals (Living Archive Seal, AI Legion Seal, Scrollfarm Mirror Seal)** | **BLOCKED — titles only, not in hand.** Download from Meta AI first. Then: simplify to **≤5 solid thread colours**, thicken every stroke to ≥0.05 in, remove fine detail and any text under 0.3 in, and get a sew-out before committing. Once redrawn, the seal becomes the chest mark for the next embroidery designs and the patch mark. |
| **Lyran Lion** | **Needs isolating + redrawing.** No isolated vector exists; the lion lives inside full-scene art. A designer must isolate and simplify it to bold solid shapes (no mane detail, no gradients) before it can be digitized. Do not embroider the full-scene lion. |
| **Kun Fayakun — Arabic calligraphy (كُن فَيَكُونُ)** | **High risk — redraw + test, large sizes only.** Calligraphy has thin strokes and diacritics that breach the 0.05-in / 0.3-in minimums at small sizes. The Arabic stays on print (poster, back DTG) in the meantime. |

These are the DTG/back-print designs — the hoodie's back in §4 is the correct use. Never send a full-scene PNG to a digitizer expecting embroidery. |
| **Saffron Trio / Token 1111 coin art** | **Redraw as a mark.** The coin is a detailed scene; the *idea* (three saffron elements / "1111") redraws well as a bold mark. The scene itself does not embroider. |

### 5.3 File requirements — POD vs local

**POD (Printful / Printify / Gelato):**
- Upload **PNG or PDF** (Printful: PNG or PDF only). **You cannot upload your own DST/PES/EXP stitch file** — the provider digitizes it, and the digitized file stays with the provider.
- Supply the artwork as **vector (AI/EPS/PDF) or high-res PNG at the true physical size** of the placement (cap front ~4–5 in wide × 2–2.5 in tall; chest 4×4 in; cuff per the mockup generator). DPI matters far less than true size — give the digitizer the real dimensions.
- **≤6 thread colours** per design (Printful unlimited-colour option: ~+$3.50–$3.95, 50+ products, no metallic/neon). All colours must come from the provider's **fixed 15-colour palette** — map brand gold / saffron / teal / cream / black to the nearest palette threads and **confirm from the physical sample**, never from a screen. Thread gold will not match screen gold exactly.
- Respect the minimums in §1 (text ≥0.25/0.3 in, strokes ≥0.05 in, clearance ≥1–1.5 mm) and the stitch ceilings (chest 15,000 / large 20,000 / hats 10,000 / sleeve 8,000). **Design target for this line: under 10,000 stitches, ≤3 thread colours.**
- Digitization takes up to ~36 hrs (Printify) / ~24 hrs (Gelato); Printful digitizes on product creation. A design that exceeds the stitch ceiling is rejected or re-quoted — build in time for one revision.

**Local shop:**
- Accepts **DST / PES / EMB** stitch files directly — and if you supply one, digitizing is usually free. Accepts **AI / EPS / PDF vector or high-res PNG** if you don't have a stitch file, and digitizes in-house ($10–$60 one-time, §2.4).
- **You keep the DST file.** That is the strategic difference: a locally digitized "1111" can be stitched by any shop, on any garment, forever. A Printful-digitized "1111" cannot leave Printful.
- Always get a **sew-out** (physical test stitch) before a batch. It is free at the better shops and is where bad digitizing is caught.

### 5.4 The designer pass (one job, before digitizing)

1. Download the three SVG seals from Meta AI (Chais — only he can do this).
2. Draw vector wordmarks: **ScrollVerse, XLVIIIblocks, 1111, 963Hz, NŪR/NUR** — bold sans, strokes ≥0.05 in at embroidery size, no thin serifs.
3. Simplify one seal (AI Legion Seal first — it is the chest/cap mark) to ≤5 solid colours, thickened strokes, no text under 0.3 in.
4. Redraw the patch mark (§3.2) as a 3-in circular design with the number field left clear for the local shop to number per piece.
5. Re-check all of it against the style guide when it lands (§0).

## 6. The one-off lane — what the local shop is for

POD covers the numbered runs. The local shop covers everything POD cannot:

- **1-of-1 pieces:** a single jacket, vest, or garment Chais supplies — tour-night pieces, gifts, the archive piece (001/144 garments can be finished locally with the patch sewn on rather than included loose). Price shape at one listed shop: ≤6,000 stitches at 1–5 pcs = **$12.00/piece decoration + digitizing $15/colour (free if you supply the DST)** + the blank Chais supplies. Budget **~$30–$50 all-in per 1-of-1** on top of the blank, plus a sew-out.
- **The numbered patches:** one batch of 144 per design from the local shop (see §3.2). At 144 units the per-piece decoration falls to ~$4.20 (≤6,000 stitches at one listed shop) — a 144-patch batch is roughly **144 × ~$4.20 ≈ $605 decoration + digitizing ~$15–$60 + patch blanks**, i.e. **~$650–$750 per design's patch batch**, or ~$4.50–$5.20 per patch. ⚠️ That is an estimate from one shop's published table — get a real quote per design; patch blank, merrowed border, and numbering method (woven number vs embroidered number) all move it. **Do not order a 144-patch batch until the POD garment sample is approved** — the patch must match the garment's thread gold.
- **Tour / event pieces:** same-day / 48-hr shops (NYC example) for last-minute needs — a capability POD (2–10 days) does not have.
- **Repairs and finishing:** sewing a patch onto a garment, fixing a stitch, adding a name/number to a piece a customer already owns.

**Rule:** the local lane is never the store's fulfilment for open-edition products — it is the numbering finisher and the 1-of-1 maker. Open editions stay POD so there is no inventory.

## 8. Risks + open questions

1. **Beanie base disagreement:** $11.95 (May 2026) vs $13.05 (16 Nov 2026 sync). Planned at $13.05 (conservative). Verify in the Printful dashboard.
2. **Hoodie price stacking:** $28.95 embroidered base + $5.95 back DTG is the published add-on arithmetic, not a dashboard-confirmed total. Verify before listing.

4. **Printify dad-hat variance:** $11.57–$12.50 in some comparisons vs $21.07 in a live Nov 2026 delivered-cost check. Do not price any Printify embroidery from a headline — build the product and read the dashboard.
5. **Thread gold ≠ screen gold.** The brand gold is a screen colour; thread gold is a physical thread. The sample is the only honest match check. If the nearest palette gold is wrong, the whole line's gold is wrong — catch it on the $19.94 cap sample, not on 144 hoodies.
6. **The Ū macron** may not stitch. Have the "NUR" fallback ready before digitizing.
7. **The second-parcel loop (Model A)** is a handling step per order. If it proves unworkable, the fallback is a printed certificate only (patch included loose in the POD parcel is not possible — POD cannot insert it), or Model B batching. Decide from Drop 1 evidence.
