## 0. THE HONEST MAP — WHAT EXISTS TODAY

Three facts shape everything below. They are stated first because the whole plan is built on them, not around them.

1. **The Walmart connector in this house is a BUYER's rail, not a seller's door.** The Walmart connection added 26 Nov searches Walmart's catalog, builds carts, and places orders the founder approves — shopping. Selling on Walmart is a different system entirely: **Walmart Marketplace**, entered through an application at marketplace.walmart.com and run from Seller Center. No seller account exists yet. Nothing in this blueprint pretends otherwise.
2. **Walmart Marketplace prohibits digital goods — by name, including NFTs and crypto.** Walmart's Prohibited Products Policy: Digital Goods (Marketplace Learn, last updated 11 Dec 2025) states: *"Digital goods and digitally delivered products are prohibited from sale on Walmart Marketplace."* The prohibited list names crypto assets, crypto currencies, crypto tokens, **NFTs**, eBooks, digital download songs, digital download movies/TV, downloadable games, gift cards (digital or physical), and *"redemption codes that are used to digitally download readily available products or access content through online portals."* The one carve-out that matters: **digital redemption codes included with a physical item in its original packaging are allowed** (Walmart's own example is the code inside a Blu-ray/DVD case). The digital lane on Walmart therefore has exactly one compliant shape — *the digital rides inside the physical* — and §3 builds only on shapes that respect the rule as written.
3. **Walmart does not accept cryptocurrency at checkout — in stores or online.** Walmart's own fraud guidance states it "does not accept or send crypto in stores." Crypto reaches a Walmart purchase only after it has been converted to dollars somewhere else (§5 separates the verified routes from the rumors).

The opportunity is real anyway — and it is mostly **physical and phigital**: Walmart Marketplace is a large, still-uncrowded shelf (Walmart reported U.S. marketplace net sales up more than 50% in Q2 FY27), Printify already integrates with it, and this house's standard — *every physical sale carries its full attached set* — fits inside Walmart's physical-goods rules when the set is built the way §4 builds it.

## 1. THE SELLER LANE — WALMART MARKETPLACE, AS WALMART PUBLISHES IT

*Sources: Walmart Marketplace Learn, "Before you start selling on Walmart Marketplace" (sellerhelp.walmart.com, guide updated 22 Jul 2025; requirements page current to 1 Sep 2026) · Walmart Marketplace, "How to start selling on Walmart.com: A step-by-step guide" (marketplace.walmart.com, 11 Feb 2026, read in full 26 Nov 2026) · Walmart Marketplace Pricing page + Marketplace Learn referral fee schedule for contract categories (schedule last updated 9 Mar 2026).*

### 1.1 Eligibility — Walmart's minimum requirements, verbatim in substance

- A copy of a **personal ID** for the applicant or legal representative (Walmart may add a photo-ID check)
- **Business Tax ID(s) or Business License Number — an SSN is not accepted**
- **Business entity classification** (a registered entity; the entity type and tax registration must match IRS records *exactly* — mismatch is the standard verification failure)
- **Supporting documents that verify the business name and address**
- A **history of marketplace or eCommerce success** (Walmart publishes no minimum; the house's track record is real: a live Shopify storefront with 271 products across US + Canada markets, scrollverse.co, and a distributed music catalog)
- Products with **GTIN/UPC GS1 Company Prefix numbers** — or a **GTIN exemption**, which Walmart offers for products without product IDs (private label, handmade); POD and house-made products apply through the exemption route
- A catalog that complies with the **Prohibited Products Policy**
- Fulfillment through **Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS)** or another **B2C U.S. warehouse with returns capability**, meeting Seller Performance Standards

### 1.2 The application, step by step (Walmart's own five-step onboarding)

1. **Create the seller account** at seller.walmart.com with a business email — minutes.
2. **Verify your business** — entity type, tax registration, business details, and the representative's government-issued ID. Walmart states verification runs **from a few minutes to two business days** once documents are in.
3. **Choose your payout method** — link a third-party payment processor account in Seller Center. New sellers sit under a **New Seller Payment Hold** while they build sales history; the hold window varies.
4. **Add market details** — business display name, customer service email and phone.
5. **Manage fulfillment** — shipping policy/preferences and returns settings — then **set up the catalog** (listings can take up to ~4 hours to complete setup after inventory is added).

End-to-end approval is the bottleneck, not the form: independent seller guides consistently report **~1–4 weeks** from application to selling, with well-prepared applications often completing in about 10 days. Walmart also markets a **New Seller Savings** program on its registration page — up to $75K in savings (up to $72K off base referral fees, up to $2K in WFS discounts, up to $1K in search-engine-marketing credits, up to $500 in Walmart Connect ad credits; conditions apply, figures are Walmart's own page copy, checked 26 Nov 2026).

### 1.3 Fees — no rent, a cut of the sale

- **No setup fee, no monthly fee, no listing fee.** Signing up is free.
- A **referral fee is deducted only when an item sells**, calculated on the total sales price (item + shipping and other charges). Rates from Walmart's published schedule (the categories this house would actually list in):

| Category | Referral fee |
|---|---|
| Apparel & Accessories | 5% (total ≤ $15) · 10% ($15–$20) · **15% (over $20)** |
| Books | 15% |
| Music · Video & DVD | 15% |
| Home, Kitchen, Decor & Garden | 15% |
| Electronics Accessories | 15% on the portion to $100 · 8% above |
| Everything Else | 15% |

- **WFS is a separate, optional cost** when used: a per-unit fulfillment fee (by size/weight) plus storage — priced through Walmart's WFS Cost Estimator. There is no WFS cost if the house fulfills its own orders (Printify, §2).

### 1.4 WFS (Walmart Fulfillment Services) — the basics

WFS is Walmart's end-to-end fulfillment: the seller ships inventory into Walmart's fulfillment network; Walmart stores it, picks and packs orders, ships with **"Fulfilled by Walmart" and 2-day delivery tags**, and handles customer service and returns on those orders. Walmart's registration guide states sellers using WFS see **50% GMV growth on average** for tagged items (Walmart first-party data, Jun 2024–Jun 2025 — Walmart's figure, cited as theirs). For this house, WFS is a *later* option, not the launch shape: print-on-demand has no inventory to inbound, and the house's physical music editions are made per order on the Kunaki/elasticStage rails. WFS becomes relevant only if a proven seller (e.g., a stocked cassette-USB re-run) earns the inbound freight. **The launch fulfillment answer on the application is Printify + the house's on-demand rails, with returns capability through the seller's U.S. returns setup.**

### 1.5 Category gates inside the catalog — read before listing anything

From the Prohibited Products Policy overview (Marketplace Learn, updated 5 Jun 2026), categories that require **pre-approval** before listing include: **Books** · **Software** · **Custom content** · **Jewelry, watches, precious gemstones, currency, coins and precious metals** (subject to the Marketplace FINCEN Covered Goods Policy). Consequences for the house catalog, stated plainly:

- **Physical collector coins** (the house's coin sets) fall under the coins/currency pre-approval language if categorized there — a pre-approval check is a required step before any coin rides a Walmart listing, not an assumption either way.
- **Books** (storybooks in print form) need pre-approval.
- Nothing in the house catalog is listed until its category treatment is confirmed in Seller Center.

## 3. THE THREE SETUPS

### 3.1 PHYSICAL — Printify products → Walmart Marketplace

The straight lane, and the launch lane:

- **What sells:** the existing Printify catalog — ScrollVerse apparel, posters, stickers, mugs — published from the connected Printify shop to a second store on the Walmart channel once the seller account and exemptions land. The catalog already exists (93 products); the Walmart surface is a *publish target*, not a rebuild.
- **Fulfillment:** Printify production on order (no inventory, no warehouse, no WFS freight), shipping by the print provider, returns to the house's U.S. returns setup. WFS stays on the shelf (§1.4) for a future stocked product.
- **Economics per order:** Walmart referral fee (§1.3) + Printify production/shipping cost; the margin is the house's. Prices on the Walmart surface are founder-set, like every surface.
- **The house's physical music editions** (Kunaki CD/vinyl/USB, elasticStage vinyl/CD — verified on-demand rails from the package sheet) can list on Walmart as what they are: physical media products, seller-fulfilled per order. Physical books follow the Books pre-approval (§1.5).

### 3.2 DIGITAL — what Walmart's rules actually allow, and the compliant shapes

Walmart's rule (§0.2) bans the digital product itself. Nothing in this section routes around it; these are the shapes that comply with it as written:

- **Shape 1 — Content embodied on a physical carrier (the cassette-USB model).** The product sold is a **physical object**: the cassette-USB — a storage device with the album's media physically on it, in printed packaging, shipped to the buyer's door. Nothing is emailed, downloaded, or portal-delivered by the sale; the buyer receives media the way a DVD buyer receives a film — embodied on the thing they bought. This is the house's signature format (HP-016: 100 units, LogoTech 2024) and it is a physical-goods listing, set up honestly as the physical product it is. The same shape covers CD and vinyl editions.
- **Shape 2 — The code inside the sealed package (Walmart's own carve-out).** Walmart expressly allows digital redemption codes **included with a physical item in its original packaging** (the Blu-ray/DVD model). A code printed inside a sealed physical package is inside the rule; a code delivered by email, message, or download after purchase is the prohibited shape. §3.3 uses this carve-out carefully — and §3.3 also draws the line it must not cross.
- **What never happens on Walmart:** no download links, no emailed files, no portal-access sales, no NFT or token listings, no gift cards. The pure-digital catalog (downloads, streams, mint lanes) lives on the house's own rails and the direct-catalog program — surfaces whose rules allow it.

### 3.3 PHIGITAL — the house standard, built to fit inside the policy

The house standard (Distribution package sheet, 25 Nov): **every physical or novelty item sold ships with the full attached set** — the NFT twin, the coin, the digital master, the full-resolution cover art, and the registered composition sheet. On Walmart, that standard is delivered in the compliant form:

- **In the package (physical, shippable, policy-clean):**
  - The physical item itself (Printify product, physical media, or collector edition).
  - **The physical coin**, where the set carries one — a struck physical object in the package. *Category care (§1.5): if Seller Center categorizes it under coins/currency/precious metals, the pre-approval step runs first; the coin is never listed past a gate.*
  - **The printed certificate** — the piece's certificate of authenticity as a physical insert: title, registry signature (SIG), SHA-256 fingerprint, seal line, and a **printed QR code**.
  - For media carriers (cassette-USB/CD), the **digital master and full-resolution cover ride on the device itself** — embodied, per Shape 1.
- **What the QR does — and does not do:** the QR points to the piece's **public registry record** — verification information anyone may read (the registry entry, the seal, the twin record). Scanning it *proves*; it does not *deliver*. It is printed matter carrying information, the same species as the fingerprint printed beside it. **The QR is never a download key and never a redemption code for digitally delivered product on the Walmart lane** — that is the prohibited shape, and the house does not use it there.
- **Where the NFT twin is issued:** on the **house's own rails**, under the standing mint gates (founder's start call, funded wallet — the Piece 0 standard). The physical buyer's twin is bound in the twin registry at the registry step (Lattice Formula Step F) and issued/claimed through house surfaces (scrollverse.co, the show-load model for key holders) — never sold, listed, or delivered through Walmart. The Walmart buyer buys a physical product with a verifiable provenance record; the digital twin attaches through the house system, exactly as it does for a buyer at a show.
- **The composition sheet** rides as a printed insert (the registered writer/publisher record + fingerprint) — paper in the package, the full sheet's registry entry one scan away.

That is the innovative use of Printify the founder pointed at: Printify's packaging and print surfaces carry the physical half (the product, and printed inserts where the provider supports them), the registry carries the proof, and the house rails carry the digital twin — three systems, one object, every rule respected as written.

## 4. WALMART CONNECT — THE LATER STAGE (one paragraph)

Walmart Connect is Walmart's retail-media advertising business — the discovery layer on top of the shelf. Its entry product is **Sponsored Search**: Sponsored Products ads (open to Marketplace sellers; the advertised item must be published on Walmart.com, in stock, and winning the Buy Box; cost-per-click, no setup or monthly fees), and Sponsored Brands / Sponsored Videos (brand owners only — a USPTO-registered trademark and onboarding to the Walmart Brand Portal). Beyond search sit Onsite Display, Brand Shop placements, and offsite/streaming offerings for larger advertisers (Marketplace Learn, "Advertise with Walmart Connect — Sponsored Search," updated 1 Oct 2026). For this house, Connect is a **stage-two lever**: it opens only after the seller account is live and listings hold the Buy Box, brand-format eligibility waits on a registered trademark the house does not yet hold, and Walmart's own new-seller offer dangles up to $500 in Connect ad credits (§1.2). **Any ad spend is the founder's word (§7)** — this blueprint budgets nothing.

## 5. DIGITAL CURRENCY OPTIONS — IN ALL WAYS, WITH THE FACTS SEPARATED

### 5.1 At Walmart — verified fact vs. rumor

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Walmart accepts Bitcoin/crypto at checkout, in store or online | **FALSE as of 26 Nov 2026.** Walmart's registers and Walmart.com take cash, cards, Walmart Pay, gift cards (and online, PayPal/Affirm). Walmart's own fraud guidance: it "does not accept or send crypto in stores." |
| "Walmart is rolling out XRP (or Litecoin) payments" | **RUMOR, recycled.** The canonical case is the fake 2021 Litecoin press release; no direct crypto checkout has ever been confirmed. Any headline of this shape is treated as unverified until Walmart's own pages say it. |
| OnePay (the fintech majority-owned by Walmart and Ribbit Capital) offers crypto | **VERIFIED — as a finance app, not a checkout.** OnePay added Bitcoin and Ether trading through Zerohash (announced Oct 2025, live by early 2026; more coins announced Mar 2026). To spend at Walmart, the user **sells crypto to dollars inside OnePay first**; Walmart receives an ordinary dollar payment. |
| Walmart gift cards can be bought with crypto | **VERIFIED — buyer-side, off-Walmart.** Third-party gift-card stores (BitPay's directory lists Walmart gift cards; CoinGate and others sell them) take crypto and issue a normal Walmart gift card, spendable like any other. Note the mirror rule: sellers may **not** sell gift cards on Marketplace (prohibited, §0.2). |
| Bitcoin kiosks inside some Walmart stores | **VERIFIED — third parties, not Walmart.** Coinstar/Coinme kiosks in some locations sell bitcoin for cash at high fees; they are separate businesses renting floor space. |

|---|---|---|
| **Walmart.com / Walmart stores (as a buyer)** | Cards, cash, Walmart Pay, gift cards, PayPal/Affirm online | **No direct crypto.** Crypto reaches a purchase only pre-converted: a gift card bought with crypto off-Walmart, or OnePay dollars after an in-app sale. |

The honest summary: **on Walmart, digital currency is a buyer's pre-conversion story, never a seller's rail.** In the house's own surfaces, crypto options exist exactly where the house built them (the mint rails, at the gate) and are available-but-unbuilt where a processor would have to be switched on (the storefronts) — each switch is the founder's word.

## 7. PROCESS TO ALL — THE SAME PACKAGE, EVERY CHANNEL

The founder's doctrine for this lane: **take the process to all, and see what lands and expands.** The Walmart build is one instance of a four-shape pattern; the pattern is the portable asset. One paragraph per shape:

**Shape A — POD merch (Printify engine, any shelf).** A design becomes a product once in Printify and publishes to every connected shelf — Shopify today, Walmart on the founder's word, and the same *Add new store* motion reaches Etsy, eBay, Amazon (US), TikTok Shop (US), and the other channels Printify lists. The work is done once; the shelves multiply. What travels: the product data, the seal-bearing descriptions, the mockups. What never changes: production on order, zero inventory, the house keeps the margin after each shelf's fee.

**Shape B — Physical media carrier (content embodied on the object).** The cassette-USB pattern — album, art, and files physically on a device in printed packaging — is a physical-goods listing on any marketplace whose rules, like Walmart's, sell objects rather than downloads. The same unit lists on the house storefront, at shows (where it doubles as the membership key, loaded on site), and on any shelf that takes physical media. What travels: the object and its packaging. What never changes: nothing is delivered digitally by the sale, anywhere.

**Shape C — Physical + printed twin certificate (the phigital insert).** Any physical product on any channel can carry the printed certificate insert — registry signature, SHA-256 fingerprint, seal line, verification QR — because printed paper is compliant everywhere physical goods are sold. The digital twin itself is always issued on house rails under the mint gates, so the insert pattern never depends on a channel's digital-goods policy: channels that ban digital delivery still take the physical product and its paper. What travels: the certificate design and the registry behind it. What never changes: the QR proves; it does not deliver.

**Shape D — Pure digital (house rails only).** Downloads, streams, and minted sets live where the house controls the rules: scrollverse.co, the direct-catalog platforms, the distribution lanes, the mint rails. This shape never forces itself onto a marketplace that prohibits it — the discipline of Shapes A–C is precisely that each channel gets the shape its rules allow, and the full world assembles on the house's own ground.

Carried together, the four shapes mean no channel is ever asked to break its rules and no part of the set is ever left behind: the shelf sells what it may, the package carries what it can, and the registry binds all of it to one record.
