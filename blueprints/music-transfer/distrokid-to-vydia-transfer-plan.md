## §1 — Transfer mechanics (researched 17 Nov 2026)

**The one rule: deliver first, take down second. Never the reverse.**

- **Streams follow the recording, not the distributor.** Spotify links the old and new copies when the audio and metadata match — same ISRC, title, artist name, duration. Apple Music groups plays across distributors. The identifiers are what the stores match on; the distributor is just the delivery company.
- **Reuse the original ISRC for every track and the original UPC for every release, and set the original release date — not today's date.** If the ISRC field is left blank, the new distributor auto-assigns a fresh code and the stores treat it as a brand-new recording with a count of zero. DistroKid's own guidance says an ISRC stays the same despite a change of distributor, ownership, or file type.
- **Metadata must match exactly:** titles, version tags, explicit status, track order, release date, track length, artist spellings, featured credits. Every field that differs lowers the chance of linking. Do not remaster, remix, or re-record anything as part of the move — a materially changed recording is a new recording and gets a new ISRC.
- **Overlap is normal and required.** While both copies are live, Spotify usually shows the duplicate quietly behind a "1 more release" link on the artist page. Wait ~5 days after the new copy goes live, check the play count matches on the desktop app, and only then request the takedown. Identity Music's guide puts full track-linking at ~6 weeks; a one-week buffer after verification costs nothing.
- **Takedown timing (DistroKid's own help pages, Sep 2026):** removals take 1–2 weeks on average — Spotify 2–5 days, Apple Music/iTunes 1–7 days, YouTube Music ~1–3 days, Amazon ~5–7 days, Deezer 1–2 weeks, Tidal ~1–2 weeks. Plan batches around the slowest store.
- **No guarantee exists.** Spotify calls track-linking "never 100% guaranteed" and DistroKid says it cannot guarantee play counts or playlist placements — the final matching is controlled by the stores. YouTube Music keeps playlist/library placement when ISRC + UPC match; for Spotify, Symphonic's guidance is that once two versions link, the new one stays on the playlists after the old one comes down. Apple Music has published nothing on playlist placement after a switch — verify playlists by hand after each batch.
- **If a release drops off Spotify mid-move, Spotify keeps its all-time stream count for 90 days.** After that it does not come back. This is why the gap — not the switch — is the danger.
- **What stays behind at DistroKid:** the store listings themselves (re-created by the new delivery), DistroKid-assigned UPCs (new distributor assigns its own — note the old ones for the record), subscription features (Splits config, promo pages), and any Leave a Legacy fees already paid (they attach to releases parked at DistroKid).
- **What travels:** the recordings and artwork (distribution is a delivery licence, not a sale of masters), the ISRCs (for life), earnings already owed (paid even after cancellation, per DistroKid policy), Spotify for Artists / Apple Music for Artists access (tied to the artist profile, not the distributor), and play counts when the redelivery matches.
- **Leave a Legacy is the wrong tool for this move.** $29 per single / $49 per album (Jul 2026) keeps a release up at DistroKid after cancellation — but it parks the catalog at DistroKid. Redelivering through Vydia with the same ISRCs keeps the music live AND moves it, with no per-release fee. Leave a Legacy only makes sense for back catalog he wants live but does not want to migrate — none of that is in this plan.
- **AI disclosure:** DistroKid's help center accepts AI-created music when the uploader holds the rights and follows rules against infringement, impersonation, and mass-generated spam. Spotify requires a "Synthetic Content" flag set through the distributor on upload. Disclose AI involvement on every release where it applies — unflagged AI content can be removed and repeat violations can suspend the whole artist profile. Never pay for guaranteed streams or playlist placement; Spotify strips artificial streams, withholds the royalties, and charges the distributor per track for flagrant cases.

**Sources:** DistroKid help center (via audora.music/guides/leaving-distrokid/, updated 2026-11-11); audora.music/guides/switch-music-distributors/; Spotify Community track-linking threads; identitymusic.com/blog/how-to-move-your-music-from-distrokid-to-identity-music; air.io label switching guide; unchainedmusic.io; thedebutroom.com/distribution/distrokid-alternatives/.

## §6 — Risks and mitigations

| Risk | What happens | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| DistroKid fee lapses mid-transfer | DistroKid controls takedown timing; releases can come down before Vydia versions link; 90-day Spotify count clock starts | Settle $103.88 FIRST (§0 step 1); keep account in good standing throughout |
| DistroKid account terminated mid-transfer | Permanent closure, dashboard blocked immediately, royalties withheld ($200–$61,000 in documented 2025–2026 cases), zero documented successful reinstatements (phase-3 law research §5.4) | Export everything FIRST; do not give cause (no ToS breach, no artificial streaming, no rights disputes); move fast once started; keep the rescue draft ready |
| ISRC/UPC mismatch or blank ISRC | Stores treat the Vydia copy as a new recording; counts reset to zero; playlists lost | Export pass (§0 step 2); never let Vydia assign new ISRCs; pilot first; verify linking before each takedown |
| Vydia assigns its own ISRCs | Reported lock-in: the identifier becomes associated with Vydia and the recording is hard to move again | Supply DistroKid ISRCs on every upload; confirm Vydia accepts them before the pilot |
| "Permanent forever" is not in the agreement | Music comes down if the Vydia agreement ends (Vydia's published standard) | Read the agreement before claiming permanence; if it is not guaranteed, say so plainly and plan accordingly |
| 80/20 vs 90/10 unresolved | Wrong split assumed; money or expectations mis-set | Confirm from the Vydia dashboard/agreement (§2); do not state 90/10 as fact until then |
| SESAC vs ASCAP publishing unresolved | Wrong publishing data on 58 uploads; disputes; collaborator shares unrecorded | Resolve §3 in the export pass; record every collaborator's publishing share |
| Feature releases uploaded without agreement | Rights dispute with Street Ka$h / Shawn Rock / Rizz-Burna / Nu Jerzey Devil; takedown of the Vydia copy | Phase 3 only with agreement; never upload another artist's primary release alone |
| Duplicate titles (Late Night / Late night, etc.) | Same song uploaded twice; split counts; confused linking | Resolve from the dashboard export; one upload per ISRC |
| Vydia plan/storage lapses | Uploads blocked or queued mid-transfer | Confirm plan + storage tier (§0 step 6) before the pilot |
| Playlist placements lost despite linking | Spotify/Apple do not guarantee playlist carry-over | Verify playlists by hand after every batch; keep the old copy live until verified |
